\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}
In this paper, we presented \nsi, the first index that answers every cell of a bounded $(r,s)$-nucleus plane from one structure.
%
The clique quotient collapses scored cliques into patterns, coefficients replace witness enumeration, and two transfer certificates reduce the whole plane to a single peeled cell.
%
The certified part of the plane is reconstructed at query time, so the index stores the quotient and the residue and nothing else.
%
On real graphs the index is up to seven orders of magnitude smaller than the explicit table it replaces, and it answers point queries in $74$ nanoseconds.
%
Its builder outperforms the state of the art by up to $742\times$ in time and $226\times$ in memory on matched cells.
%
One structure answers every cell of the plane.
