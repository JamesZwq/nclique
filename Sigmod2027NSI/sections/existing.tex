In this section, we recall the peeling skeleton that all existing algorithms instantiate, and we measure where its cost concentrates.

\stitle{The peeling skeleton.}
Every existing algorithm computes one cell $(r,s)$ by the same skeleton, shown in Algorithm~\ref{alg:explicit} \cite{firstNucleus,NuclearCD}.
%
It materializes the scored cliques, initializes their supports, and repeatedly removes a clique of minimum support.
%
The level $k$ never decreases, and each clique receives the level at which it is removed.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{The explicit peel of existing algorithms.}
\label{alg:explicit}
\algfontsize
\KwIn{Graph $G$, cell $(r,s)$}
\KwOut{$\kappa_s(R)$ for every $r$-clique $R$ of $G$}
$\mathcal{R} \leftarrow$ all $r$-cliques of $G$\tcp*{one record per clique}\label{ln:enum}
\ForEach{$R \in \mathcal{R}$}{
    $sup(R) \leftarrow \deg_{(s)}(R)$\label{ln:supinit}
}
$k \leftarrow 0$\;
\While{$\mathcal{R} \neq \emptyset$}{\label{ln:loop}
    $R^{*} \leftarrow$ a clique of minimum $sup$ in $\mathcal{R}$\label{ln:min}\;
    $k \leftarrow \max(k,\, sup(R^{*}))$;\quad $\kappa_s(R^{*}) \leftarrow k$\label{ln:assign}\;
    \ForEach{witness $S \supseteq R^{*}$ not yet destroyed}{\label{ln:witness}
        \ForEach{$R' \subset S$ with $R' \in \mathcal{R},\ R' \neq R^{*}$}{
            $sup(R') \leftarrow sup(R') - 1$\label{ln:dec}
        }
    }
    remove $R^{*}$ from $\mathcal{R}$\label{ln:remove}\;
}
\Return{$\kappa_s$}
\end{algorithm}

Algorithm~\ref{alg:explicit} enumerates the scored cliques and allocates one mutable record per clique (Line~\ref{ln:enum}).
%
It initializes each support (Line~\ref{ln:supinit}) and then peels: extract a minimum (Line~\ref{ln:min}), assign the current level (Line~\ref{ln:assign}), and credit every scored clique that loses a witness (Lines~\ref{ln:witness}--\ref{ln:dec}).
%
The state of the art, \cnd~\cite{NuclearCD}, realizes Lines~\ref{ln:supinit} and \ref{ln:witness}--\ref{ln:dec} by aggregated counting, so no witness clique is ever listed one by one.
%
This counting is what made single cells with millions of edges practical, and we adopt it as our baseline throughout.

\stitle{Cost anatomy.}
The skeleton's remaining cost sits in Line~\ref{ln:enum}: every scored clique of the cell exists as a record, and every record carries mutable peel state.
%
Memory is therefore at least proportional to the number of scored cliques, and time to the stream of per-clique updates.
%
Both grow combinatorially with $r$, as Table~\ref{tab:objects} shows for \webit.

The second column of Table~\ref{tab:objects} counts the objects of Line~\ref{ln:enum}; the third charges a conservative eight bytes per record.
%
At $r=4$ the records alone exceed the memory of a large server, and at $r=5$ they exceed it by two orders of magnitude.
%
The measured behavior matches: on \webit, \cnd{} spends $5{,}581$ seconds and $101$\,GB on the single shallow cell $(3,4)$.
%
Within a $1{,}800$-second, $300$\,GB budget, it finishes none of the nine cells of our evaluation grid (Section~\ref{sec:experiments}).

\stitle{What a plane demands.}
The skeleton is also strictly per cell: nothing computed at $(r,s)$ is reused at $(r,s{+}1)$ or $(r{+}1,s)$.
%
A plane therefore multiplies the cost by its number of cells.
%
Aggregated counting removed the witness side of the cost; the scored side, one record per clique per cell, remains.
%
Removing it requires a representation in which many scored cliques share one record and all cells share one representation.
%
Building that representation is the subject of the next section.
