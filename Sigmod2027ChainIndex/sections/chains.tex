\section{Chains: Hierarchy Equivalence}\label{sec:chains}

The redundancy of \strees is on the vertex axis: a vertex is stored once per size although many vertices are indistinguishable by every query.
This section makes that precise.

\begin{definition}[chain]\label{def:chain}
For a vertex $v$ with $\omg{v}\ge2$ let $\chain{v}=(\own{2}{v},\own{3}{v},\dots,\own{\omg{v}}{v})$ be the tuple of its own nodes over the sizes it participates in.
The vertices with equal tuples form a \emph{chain}.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[chains are hierarchy equivalence]\label{thm:chains}
For $u,v$ with $\omg{u},\omg{v}\ge2$ the following are equivalent:
(a) $\chain{u}=\chain{v}$;
(b) $\core{s}{u}=\core{s}{v}$ for every $s$, and for every $s$ and every $1\le k\le\core{s}{u}$ the vertices $u$ and $v$ lie in the same $\Nuc{s}{k}$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
(a)$\Rightarrow$(b): $\own{s}{u}=\own{s}{v}$ gives $\core{s}{u}=\mathrm{top}(\own{s}{u})=\core{s}{v}$; for $k\le\core{s}{u}$ the $\Nuc{s}{k}$ containing $u$ is the unique ancestor-or-self of $\own{s}{u}$ whose level interval contains $k$, the same node for $v$.
(b)$\Rightarrow$(a): with $\core{s}{u}=\core{s}{v}=k$, the $\Nuc{s}{k}$ containing $u$ is $\own{s}{u}$ by definition; it contains $v$ and is therefore the $\Nuc{s}{\core{s}{v}}$ containing $v$, that is $\own{s}{v}$.
\end{proof}

Condition (b) says that no value query and no community query distinguishes $u$ from $v$.
An index over chains therefore loses nothing, and the answers stay whole:

\begin{lemma}[communities are unions of chains]\label{lem:union}
If $u$ lies in an $\Nuc{s}{k}$ $N$ with $k\ge1$ and $\chain{w}=\chain{u}$, then $w\in N$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
$\core{s}{w}=\core{s}{u}\ge k$, and $\own{s}{w}=\own{s}{u}\subseteq N$ because the top of $\own{s}{u}$ is $\core{s}{u}\ge k$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[twins refine chains]\label{lem:twins}
If $u$ and $w$ have the same closed neighbourhood then $\chain{u}=\chain{w}$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The transposition of $u$ and $w$ is an automorphism of $G$; it preserves every core value and maps every nucleus to a nucleus containing the other vertex, so condition (b) holds.
\end{proof}

Chains are much coarser than twin classes on real graphs: twin classes have 1.03 to 1.28 vertices on average on our inputs, while \dblp has 13,459 chains for 317,080 vertices and \webuk has 839 chains for 129,632 vertices.

\begin{proposition}[the coarsest lossless partition]\label{prop:coarsest}
Call a partition $P$ of $V$ lossless if every community of every size and level is a union of blocks of $P$.
The chain partition is lossless, and every lossless partition is at least as fine: each of its blocks lies inside one chain.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Losslessness of chains is Lemma~\ref{lem:union}.
Let $P$ be lossless and let $u,v$ share a block of $P$; we show $\chain{u}=\chain{v}$.
By Theorem~\ref{thm:chains} it suffices to show $\own{s}{u}=\own{s}{v}$ at every size.
If $\core{s}{u}>\core{s}{v}$, the community of $u$ at $(s,\core{s}{u})$ contains $u$ and not $v$, and is not a union of blocks.
If $\core{s}{u}=\core{s}{v}=k$ and $\own{s}{u}\ne\own{s}{v}$, these two $(s,k)$-nuclei are disjoint, so the community of $u$ at $(s,k)$ again contains $u$ and not $v$.
Hence the own nodes agree.
\end{proof}
So an exact index cannot store fewer than the chains, and storing more than the chains stores nothing that any answer needs; the vertex-to-chain map is the part of the index that no exact representation can avoid.

\stitle{Relation to other atomic units.}
The SGL index for bipartite bi-components \cite{SGL} also builds its index from cohesive units that are neither nested nor overlapping, so that each vertex is retrieved once per query; there, however, a vertex belongs to one unit per non-dominated parameter pair, so the units overlap and a vertex is stored several times.
In the $(1,s)$-nucleus setting the per-size laminarity together with Theorem~\ref{thm:chains} makes the units a partition: each vertex is stored once and retrieved once.

\stitle{How many chains.}
Chains are at most as many as vertices and at most as many as twin classes.
They are not bounded by the number of canonical nodes: two vertices with the same size-3 node and different size-2 nodes, together with two more vertices in a second size-3 node sharing those two size-2 nodes, give four chains on five nodes, and stacking the construction gives $2^t$ chains on $2t+1$ nodes.
On our graphs the ratio of chains to nodes ranges from 0.26 to 5.67, so the saving is empirical: it is the number of vertices per chain, and Section~\ref{sec:exp} shows that this single quantity predicts the compression.
