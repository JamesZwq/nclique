\section{Related Work}\label{sec:related}

\stitle{Nucleus decompositions and their hierarchies.}
Sariy{\"u}ce et al.\ introduced the $(r,s)$-nucleus decomposition and the hierarchy of nuclei of one parameter pair \cite{firstNucleus,nuclearTWEB}, and gave a construction of that hierarchy from the peel order that avoids repeated traversals \cite{fastHierarchy}; local \cite{LocalNuclear} and parallel \cite{sotaPrveHierarchy,sotaHierarchy} algorithms followed, the last computing the hierarchy of one pair in parallel.
Clique counting by pivoting \cite{Pivoter,YeSIGMOD2022Lightning} counts the $s$-cliques of every size from one succinct tree without listing them; the counting-based decomposition \cnd \cite{Zhang2026NucleusCounting} peels one size from a clique path index that it rewrites as vertices leave, and a later single-size peel keeps that index fixed and updates only the counts.
All of these compute the values, and at most the hierarchy, of one size per run.
The present paper stores every size's hierarchy for querying, from one all-size peel that shares the clique tree across sizes; Section~\ref{sec:exp} measures that peel against \cnd run once per size.

\stitle{Community search and its indexes.}
Community search asks for the cohesive subgraph of a query vertex \cite{SozioGionisKDD2010,Cui2014Local}; the survey of Fang et al.\ \cite{Fang2020Survey} covers the models.
Indexes for the $k$-core model store the core hierarchy of the graph as a tree of nested cores, the ShellStruct and CL-tree structures \cite{Barbieri2015ShellStruct,Fang2016CLtree}, and answer a query by locating the query vertex's node and walking up to the requested $k$; the size-2 layer of our index is such a tree, and Theorem~\ref{thm:onerange} says that under our labels each of its communities is one label range.
For the $k$-truss model, TCP-index \cite{huang2014truss} keeps a maximum spanning forest per vertex and EquiTruss \cite{akbas2017truss} partitions the edges into truss-equivalence classes, edges that lie in the same $k$-truss communities for every $k$, so that a community is a union of classes and each edge is stored once.
Chains are the same idea taken across sizes: two vertices are hierarchy-equivalent when they share every community at every size and every level, and Theorem~\ref{thm:chains} shows that this equivalence is exactly the equality of own nodes, which is what makes the partition computable from the per-size hierarchies and the index over it lossless.
Indexes over a second axis, time in historical and temporal core queries \cite{core_res1,tian2024abcore}, store versions of one hierarchy; the axis here is the clique size, along which the hierarchies nest (the nesting of Theorem~\ref{thm:tail}) instead of changing arbitrarily.
The SGL index for bi-components of bipartite graphs \cite{SGL} builds its index from units that are neither nested nor overlapping so that each vertex is retrieved once per query; a vertex there belongs to one unit per non-dominated parameter pair, and so is stored several times, while chains are a partition.

\stitle{Kruskal--Katona.}
The certified tail (Theorem~\ref{thm:tail}) rests on the shadow theorem of Kruskal and Katona \cite{kruskal1963,katona1968}, which bounds the number of $(s-1)$-cliques through a vertex by the number of $s$-cliques through it.
